Raising a corpse only needs the corpse itself — no [[Pacts]] deal — but success isn't guaranteed. The chance depends on how the corpse's level compares to the necromancer's own: a corpse at your level succeeds 2 times in 10, following (2/10)^(corpse level − your level + 1). Each level the corpse sits above you multiplies the difficulty by another 2/10, while a corpse even one level below you already clears the formula past 100%, making the attempt a guaranteed success.

Like every other [[Class]], becoming a Necromancer isn't just a matter of learning the [[Curse]] — it means acquiring something tied to the practice's progenitor, and that progenitor isn't a Spirit or a god. It's the very first mortal who ever became a [[Lich]], proving a corpse and Spirit could be bound into undeath at all rather than simply returned through [[Life]] or [[Death]] outright.
- **Primary** — an actual piece of the First Lich's remains, vanishingly rare since a Lich rarely dies cleanly enough to leave anything behind to find. Its unique ability works the same way [[Character Death]] already does for anyone — the wielder keeps playing as the same character instead of losing them, and chooses which stat takes the -1 instead of it always hitting their highest — except the loss isn't permanent: it heals back the next time they successfully raise a corpse of their own.
- **Secondary** — one of the First Lich's own creations, some ancient [[Death knight]] or [[Dullahan]] still bound to its will centuries later. Its unique ability binds that specific undead directly to the necromancer instead of raising a new one, handing over a subordinate that kept its full skill rather than the fraction any freshly-raised corpse would.
- **Tertiary** — everything else: any ordinary [[Corpse]], [[Bones]], or [[Curse]]-inscribed material, which is why nearly every necromancer alive today is building their Class on nothing better than this.
