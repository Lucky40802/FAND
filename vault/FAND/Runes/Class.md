To gain a class tied to a specific Law, a mortal has to acquire something closely bound to that Law's progenitor — the god or Spirit the Law actually comes from — rather than simply learning to cast it. What's acquired falls into three tiers, and the closer the tier, the more the class's power grows:

- **Primary** — a direct piece of the progenitor itself: a body part, blood, or fragment of the entity the Law was pulled from. A shard of [[Diamond]]'s shattered body would count as Primary toward a Diamond class, the same way a piece of [[Ignis]] itself would toward Fire.
- **Secondary** — something once removed from the progenitor: an item it made, wore, or passed down to a child Law rather than the entity itself. A relic of [[Iron]], [[Gnome]]'s first child, would count as Secondary toward an Earth-lineage class rather than Primary.
- **Tertiary** — everything else with a thematic link but no real lineage to the progenitor: an object simply shaped from or touched by the Law, like an ordinary [[Blade]] forged from [[Steel]] rather than anything pulled from [[Iron]] or [[Gnome]] directly.

Primary acquisitions are rare enough that most are gone with their progenitors or locked away — [[Iron]] himself is still stuck in the spirit realm, for instance — which is why almost every class an ordinary mortal ends up with is built on nothing better than a Tertiary link.

Tertiary only raises a class's power, since there's rarely enough of the progenitor left in it to shape into anything specific. Primary and Secondary are different: the exact piece acquired unlocks an ability unique to it, so no two Primary or Secondary relics grant quite the same thing.
